\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

This chapter presents the research context, central problem statement, and architectural foundation of the SENTINEL system---a Cognitive Two-Tier Architecture designed to automate threat detection and incident response within Security Operations Centers (SOCs). By critically analyzing the structural limitations of conventional deterministic defenses, the alert fatigue crisis, and the latency bottlenecks and adversarial security vulnerabilities inherent in Large Language Models (LLMs), this chapter establishes the research objectives, strategic research questions, scope, Design Science Research methodology, and core academic contributions of this thesis.

\section{Background and Motivation}

The expansion of enterprise IT infrastructure alongside the growing sophistication of multi-vector cyberattack campaigns poses severe operational challenges for security monitoring. Within modern Security Operations Centers (SOCs), Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) platforms are tasked with ingesting and aggregating millions of telemetry log events daily~\cite{siem2021}. Conventional defense mechanisms rely heavily on perimeter filtration devices operating on static rulesets or fixed signatures. This approach exhibits fundamental limitations against novel evasion techniques and obfuscated attack variants. To minimize the risk of missing critical threats, security configurations frequently broaden sensitivity thresholds, resulting in hundreds of thousands of daily alerts with false positive rates exceeding 80\%~\cite{alertfatigue2022}. Consequently, security analysts suffer from cognitive exhaustion (alert fatigue), severely compromising their ability to identify genuine intrusion events. Concurrently, legacy Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response (SOAR) platforms operate on pre-scripted playbooks, completely lacking adaptive contextual reasoning capabilities~\cite{soar2025}.

Integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) offers a promising avenue toward automating security context synthesis and reasoning. Nevertheless, deploying LLMs directly into live network telemetry streams presents three core technical barriers:
First, the computational complexity of the self-attention mechanism causes inference latencies ranging from 4 to 26 seconds per event, incurring severe data pipeline congestion at wire-speed~\cite{vaswani2017}.
Second, the adversarial security risk stemming from log-substrate prompt injection attacks, wherein malicious instructions embedded in raw log fields manipulate the model's internal reasoning flow~\cite{promptinjection2023}.
Third, stringent data sovereignty regulations and Zero-Trust architecture mandates strictly prohibit exfiltrating internal security telemetry to public cloud AI services, requiring reasoning engines to operate entirely on-premises on bounded GPU hardware~\cite{nist80207}.
To resolve these challenges, this research proposes the SENTINEL architecture, synergizing an O(1) statistical filter and a LightGBM Machine Learning Gate at Tier 1 with an Agentic AI reasoning system at Tier 2, safeguarded by cryptographic nonces and an HMAC audit trail.

\section{Research Objectives}

The overarching objective of this thesis is to design, prototypically build, and empirically evaluate the SENTINEL architecture to automate network security threat detection, contextual reasoning, and incident response on local infrastructure, achieving optimal processing latency and cryptographic security.

The general objective is operationalized through three central research tasks:
First, constructing a dual-stage stateless filtration pipeline at Tier 1 operating at wire-speed, combining Welford's O(1) online statistical algorithm, a LightGBM Machine Learning Gate, and a Tier 1.75 Semantic Cache to classify and shed at least 80\% of confident benign telemetry without consuming GPU compute resources.
Second, designing a structural protection mechanism for the Tier 2 language model via Delimited Data Encapsulation using random cryptographic nonces, an output action guardrail, and an HMAC-SHA256 log audit chain to neutralize prompt injection attacks and ensure forensic non-repudiation.
Third, developing a stateful reasoning engine based on a LangGraph agent state-graph coupled with persistent Threat Memory and a Dual-RAG retrieval system (combining FAISS vector search and BM25 sparse keyword matching) integrated with a STIX-aligned MITRE ATT\&CK knowledge base, enabling transparent forensic justification generation, detecting and neutralizing hallucination via deterministic RAG guardrails, and facilitating multi-stage intrusion correlation.

\section{Research Questions}
\label{sec:rq}

The design and empirical evaluation process of this thesis are guided by three strategic research questions:

The first research question (RQ1) addresses pipeline efficiency and filtration economics: How can a multi-tiered architecture be designed to autonomously offload security telemetry at wire-speed, thereby resolving the latency bottlenecks and resource consumption inherent in Large Language Models?

The second research question (RQ2) addresses AI security boundaries and forensic integrity: What methodologies and security mechanisms can effectively withstand adversarial risks (such as log-substrate prompt injection) to protect the AI's reasoning flow, while simultaneously ensuring the integrity and non-repudiation of forensic audit trails?

The third research question (RQ3) addresses stateful agent reasoning and technical attribution: How can stateful reasoning capabilities and domain-specific knowledge retrieval be integrated into an AI Agent to automate intrusion analysis, accurately attribute attack techniques, and generate transparent forensic reports, effectively replacing manual human intervention?

\section{Scope and Object of Study}

The primary subject of study is the SENTINEL Two-Tier Cognitive Architecture, comprising the Tier 1 stateless filter (online Welford statistics and LightGBM Machine Learning Gate), Tier 1.75 Semantic Cache, Tier 2 stateful agent engine (LangGraph state-graph and Q4\_K\_M quantised Foundation-Sec-8B-Instruct hosted locally via llama.cpp), Dual-RAG retrieval system (combining FAISS and BM25) integrated with MITRE ATT\&CK knowledge, and cryptographic security controls including Delimited Nonce Encapsulation and HMAC-SHA256 log audit chains.

The evaluation scope is constrained to benchmark datasets: CSE-CIC-IDS2018 for wide-area network telemetry, CSIC 2010 for real-world HTTP Layer 7 attacks targeting e-commerce applications, and DAPT2020 for complex APT multi-stage intrusions. The complete system is deployed and tested on a single local GPU workstation equipped with an NVIDIA RTX 4060 Ti 16GB VRAM graphics card, Intel Core i7 processor, and 32GB RAM, operating fully air-gapped to meet strict data sovereignty mandates. Automated response actions are restricted to blocking IP addresses, logging events, or escalating to human security analysts; physical infrastructure disconnection interventions are excluded from this study.

\section{Research Methodology}

This thesis adopts the Design Science Research (DSR) methodology combining theoretical formalization with quantitative empirical validation across three main pillars:
The statistical and machine learning pillar combines Welford O(1) online statistics for rolling Z-score anomaly detection with LightGBM classification models and telemetry frequency/rate distribution metrics.
The agentic logic pillar models an acyclic state-graph in LangGraph orchestrating language model reasoning, integrated with quantitative hybrid retrieval methods including FAISS vector search, BM25 sparse keyword search, and Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) rank aggregation.
The adversarial security pillar stress-tests resilience against prompt injection attacks and evaluates forensic log non-repudiation.

System efficacy is quantitatively evaluated using a 5D Metrics Framework covering: classification accuracy (Precision, Recall, F1), processing performance (latency in milliseconds and autonomous offload rate \%), prompt injection deflection security, explanation coherence scored by an independent judge model, and log audit integrity via cryptographic hashing. Controlled ablation studies are conducted to measure and isolate individual tier contributions.

\section{Core Contributions of the Thesis}

This thesis contributes three core values to the fields of cybersecurity and artificial intelligence.

In system architecture, the research proposes and proves the efficacy of the SENTINEL Two-Tier Architecture. Synergizing O(1) statistical filtering and a Machine Learning Gate at Tier 1 sheds the majority of telemetry at wire-speed with millisecond latencies, while Tier 1.75 Semantic Caching resolves repeated alerts, eliminating latency bottlenecks and preserving GPU compute resources.

In AI security, the research contributes a structural defense mechanism utilizing Delimited Data Encapsulation with random cryptographic nonces and deterministic action guardrails. This provides an architectural security guarantee, neutralizing prompt injection attacks without relying on probabilistic semantic classifiers. Concurrently, an HMAC-SHA256 signature chain guarantees non-repudiation for forensic logs.

In agentic logic and security knowledge, the research replaces rigid SOAR playbooks with a stateful LangGraph agent graph connected to a 433-item STIX-aligned MITRE ATT\&CK knowledge base and persistent Threat Memory, enabling automated correlation of complex intrusion events and transparent forensic justification generation running smoothly on consumer GPU hardware.
