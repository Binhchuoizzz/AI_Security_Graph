\chapter{Experiments and Evaluation}
\label{ch:experiments_evaluation}

This chapter evaluates SENTINEL across three core dimensions: stream offloading performance, adversarial AI security guardrails with cryptographic audit integrity, and stateful agentic reasoning quality. Experiments were executed on an air-gapped environment containerized via Docker Compose (Intel Core i7, 32\,GB RAM, local NVIDIA GPU running Foundation-Sec-8B-Instruct~\cite{foundation_sec_instruct}). Offloading performance is measured on an operational stream of 99,717 events whose 9.8\% attack base rate is consistent with the low proportion of genuine threats reported for production SOCs~\cite{alahmadi2022}, while security guardrails and classification quality are assessed across benchmark datasets (CSE-CIC-IDS2018~\cite{ids2018}, CSIC 2010~\cite{csic2010}, DAPT 2020~\cite{dapt2020}) and 823 adversarial samples~\cite{advbench2023, deepset2023, jackhhao2023, shen2024dan}. All reported metrics state their respective denominators and address the core research questions directly.

\section{Stream Offloading Performance and Filtration Economics}
\label{sec:tier1_performance}

The primary bottleneck in deploying Large Language Models (LLMs) within Security Operations Centers (SOCs) is the high inference cost and latency incurred when evaluating every telemetry event individually. To address this challenge, SENTINEL implements a filtration economics architecture guided by the core design principle: \textit{``resolve cheap adjudications deterministically before escalating to expensive reasoning''}. As defined in Section~\ref{sec:architecture_overview}, an event is successfully offloaded when it is processed at lower filtration tiers without invoking LLM inference. This metric aggregates all terminal adjudication actions including \texttt{DROP}, \texttt{BLOCK\_IP}, \texttt{ALERT}, and \texttt{AWAIT\_HITL}, as none consume LLM inference tokens.

Empirical evaluation on the operational SOC-like stream of 99,717 events at a 9.8\% attack base rate demonstrates SENTINEL's capacity for computational workload reduction. The system successfully offloads 97.5\% of total volume (eliminating 97,199 LLM invocations), escalating only 2.5\% (2,518 events) to Tier 2 for cognitive reasoning. The tiered offloading mechanism operates through close coordination among technical components. Tier 1 utilizes static signature rules combined with an $\mathcal{O}(1)$ Welford statistical algorithm~\cite{welford1962} to resolve 38.3\% of the stream at a mean latency of 0.182\,ms without GPU consumption; that share comprises 31,012 benign events and 7,195 high-volume attacks. The LightGBM Machine Learning Gate~\cite{ke2017lightgbm} acts as an intermediate filter, evaluating each 76-dimensional feature vector in 0.287\,ms (3,452 events/second) and offloading an additional 59.2\% of benign telemetry. Evaluated on \texttt{datatest.json} (4,236 events containing flow features and HTTP payloads), the Gate achieves an MCC of 0.667 and a balanced accuracy of 0.833 over the 2,534 events it adjudicates directly, together with 100\% precision (0 false positives) across 962 autonomous blocking commands (\texttt{BLOCK\_IP}). For repeated events, the Tier 1.75 Semantic Cache resolves 81.3\% of queries with an 8.9$\times$ speed-up factor. Consequently, Tier 2 receives only 2.5\% of complex events, concentrating 95.0\% of genuine threats into 15.76\% of workload and reducing analyst reading material by 84.24\%.

End-to-end latency evaluation over 500 events sampled at a fixed stride shows mean latency falling from 17,187.9\,ms to 5,286.7\,ms (a reduction of 69.2\%; Mann--Whitney $U$ test, $p \approx 9 \times 10^{-57}$). Median latency drops from 17,174.7\,ms to 0.88\,ms (nearly a 19,500$\times$ reduction) as typical benign events are resolved under 1\,ms at Tier 1. The 95th percentile latency ($P_{95}$) is 25,829\,ms (vs. 21,434\,ms under single-tier) because escalated events undergo pre-filtering before LLM processing, as shown in the Empirical Cumulative Distribution Function (ECDF) in Figure~\ref{fig:latency_ecdf}.

\section{Adversarial AI Guardrails and Cryptographic Audit Integrity}
\label{sec:adversarial_security_hmac}

Adversarial security evaluation of SENTINEL is conducted across two primary threat surfaces: language-space prompt injection and numeric feature-space evasion. At Tier 1, the system deploys a primary static signature filtering layer comprising three parallel detectors (pattern matching, encoding neutralization, and delimiter isolation) to intercept malicious instructions early. This static layer is evaluated on 823 standardized prompt injection samples aggregated from three international benchmark corpora: AdvBench~\cite{advbench2023}, deepset~\cite{deepset2023}, and jackhhao~\cite{jackhhao2023}, supplemented by DAN jailbreak variants~\cite{shen2024dan}. Experimental results show that the static filter intercepts 18.8\% (132/703 samples) of published benchmark corpora and achieves 72.5\% (58/80 samples) on author-composed samples targeted at complex obfuscation techniques. When evaluated against a negative control set of 230 benign telemetry logs, the static layer records 46 false flags (a 20.0\% rate); all 46 flags stem from the encoding detector and function strictly as enrichment metadata rather than causing denial-of-service (DoS) to legitimate traffic. For the remaining 678 complex adversarial samples escalating past Tier 1 (including 543 samples that genuinely bypassed static checks), the system routes telemetry to Tier 2. Rather than relying on probabilistic classifiers that inherently contain blind spots against novel attack patterns, Tier 2 enforces structural protection through Delimited Data Encapsulation. This mechanism encapsulates raw telemetry payloads within deterministic isolation boundaries using unique random nonces, preventing embedded instructions from overriding LLM system prompts. Evaluated over all 678 escalated samples (100\% coverage), Tier 2 achieves a 100\% mitigation rate (678/678 samples), blocking every agent-hijacking attempt within this sample set. Position-specific tests across 100 samples distributed over four payload fields (\texttt{URI}, \texttt{User-Agent}, \texttt{message}, \texttt{payload}) confirm uniform 100\% protection. When backend structural errors are deliberately induced, Pydantic schema validators safely drop the agent into an \texttt{AWAIT\_HITL} state without system collapse.

On the numeric feature-space evasion surface, 1,036 attack samples derived from CSE-CIC-IDS2018~\cite{ids2018} and CSIC 2010~\cite{csic2010} datasets undergo extreme feature distortion to stress-test the LightGBM Machine Learning Gate. The Gate enforces Z-score clipping ($\pm 8\sigma$) and an autonomous abstention policy whenever over 30\% of feature dimensions exceed $6\sigma$. Consequently, 98.75\% of attack samples (1,023/1,036) maintain correct malicious classification, while 116 anomalous events (from a 4,236 web/flow telemetry pool) are autonomously escalated to Tier 2 for deep cognitive adjudication. In parallel with adversarial guardrails, forensic audit log integrity is guaranteed via chained HMAC-SHA256 cryptographic signatures, where each log record incorporates the cryptographic hash of its predecessor. Empirical testing across 30 forgery attack trials for each tampering category (content modification, fake record insertion, and intermediate record deletion) demonstrates 100\% positional tampering detection accuracy with zero false alarms across 450 intact log entries, ensuring non-repudiation and forensic audit integrity for security operations.

\section{Agentic Cognitive Reasoning and Attack Technique Attribution}
\label{sec:tier2_accuracy_rag}

SENTINEL's cognitive reasoning capability and MITRE ATT\&CK technique attribution performance are evaluated on a 500-payload sample subset extracted from CSIC 2010~\cite{csic2010} (comprising 250 attack-labeled web application payloads and 250 benign payloads). Experimental evaluation demonstrates that the hybrid Dual-RAG knowledge retrieval module (combining FAISS semantic search~\cite{faiss2019} and BM25 keyword search~\cite{bm25_2009} via Reciprocal Rank Fusion~\cite{rrf2009}) achieves an 80.0\% exact-match retrieval ceiling (200/250 samples) at the technique level, with a Hit@3 of 0.93 measured on the 243 of those samples that escalated. However, when evaluated across the end-to-end Tier 2 agent pipeline, technique attribution accuracy records 68.0\% (170/250 samples, with tactic-level accuracy reaching 70.4\% at a median latency of 13,293\,ms). The 12.0 percentage-point drop from retrieval to end-to-end reasoning stems from the evidence-anchoring guardrail: 22 of the 30 samples lost were forced to \texttt{N/A} because the proposed technique was absent from the retrieved evidence documents. This represents an intentional architectural trade-off prioritizing ground-truth verification over ungrounded predictions. Table~\ref{tab:rq3_attribution} demonstrates the empirical divergence between the RAG retrieval ceiling and end-to-end attribution accuracy. The 12.0 percentage-point gap (falling from the 80.0\% retrieval ceiling to 68.0\% end-to-end accuracy, corresponding to 30 misattributed events) decomposes into three distinct technical mechanisms:

First, the anti-hallucination role of the Evidence-Anchoring Guardrail: out of the 30 misattributed samples, 22 cases (accounting for 73.3\% of the accuracy drop) occurred when the language model proposed an attack technique that was plausible under parametric intuition but absent from the five retrieved RAG documents injected into the context window. Rather than permitting ungrounded extrapolation, the guardrail strictly forced these decisions to \texttt{N/A} and routed them to the deferred queue (\texttt{AWAIT\_HITL}). This represents a deliberate safety trade-off: forfeiting 8.8 percentage points of nominal autonomous accuracy to guarantee that 100\% of emitted technique attributions possess verifiable evidentiary provenance, eliminating the operational hazard of automated firewall misconfigurations driven by AI hallucinations;

Second, hierarchical multi-label ambiguity inherent in web attack payloads: HTTP attack samples from CSIC 2010~\cite{csic2010} (such as SQL injection or cross-site scripting) simultaneously exhibit the characteristics of both public service exploitation (\texttt{T1190}) and client/system script execution (\texttt{T1059.007}). While the Dual-RAG module retrieves both reference documents, the ground-truth benchmark only records a single label. When the agent selects the accompanying valid technique, the single-label exact-match evaluation penalizes the decision as incorrect, bringing the full-pipeline accuracy of \texttt{T1190} to 63.5\% and \texttt{T1059.007} to 73.3\%;

Third, knowledge coverage disparity and context rank positions: for \texttt{T1083} (File and Directory Discovery, $n=12$), the retriever fails completely (Hit@3 = 0.00) due to knowledge base coverage gaps, denying the agent evidentiary grounding. Conversely, for \texttt{T1071.001} (Web Protocols), the correct document is consistently retrieved at rank 3 (Hit@3 = 1.00), but reciprocal rank fusion (RRF)~\cite{rrf2009} weights fail to elevate it to rank 1, resulting in a 0.0\% exact-match rate at both the retrieval and full-pipeline stages: a context-ranking disparity rather than a knowledge coverage failure. The reasoning quality of Foundation-Sec-8B-Instruct is evaluated on 1,052 escalated events along four RAGAS axes~\cite{ragas2023} by a judge model of a different family, Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct, following the recommended protocol against self-enhancement bias~\cite{zheng2023judge}. The evaluation yields an average score of 3.78/5.0 (answer relevance: 4.52, context recall: 4.11, faithfulness: 3.96, context precision: 2.54). Direct evidence anchoring, by contrast, reaches only 11.2\% (95\% CI 9.5--13.3\%): fewer than one justification in eight cites a log value that can be checked back against the record, which bounds the transparency of the explanation layer.

The relatively lower Context Precision score (2.54/5.0) compared to Context Recall (4.11/5.0) stems from three concrete architectural factors:
First, semantic heterogeneity within the Dual-RAG knowledge corpus: the retrieval engine simultaneously pulls technical attack definitions from MITRE ATT\&CK~\cite{strom2018mitre} and incident handling procedures from NIST SP 800-61r2~\cite{nist80061}. When the LLM judge evaluates pure attack attribution queries, all retrieved NIST passages (which detail containment, eradication, and recovery workflows) as well as adjacent sub-techniques are evaluated as non-target context, reducing the signal-to-noise ratio of the blended context window;
Second, a deliberate recall-oriented retrieval policy: within operational SOC monitoring, missing an active threat (False Negative) due to an overly restrictive retrieval threshold poses an existential security hazard far greater than retrieving auxiliary reference text. The RRF hybrid search~\cite{rrf2009} intentionally sacrifices context purity to maintain high context recall (4.11/5.0, or 82.2\%), delegating final discriminative filtering to the cognitive attention mechanism of the Tier 2 language model;
Third, lexical overlap in standard network telemetry: cybersecurity event logs feature a high density of standardized protocol tokens (\texttt{HTTP}, \texttt{POST}, status code \texttt{200}, standard headers). The BM25 keyword branch~\cite{bm25_2009} within the hybrid fusion pipeline frequently pulls in techniques that share transport-layer primitives but differ in strategic intent.

Ablation evaluation across 1,700 test samples demonstrates that the full two-tier architecture achieves an action accuracy of 35.35\% (95\% CI 33.12--37.66), significantly outperforming the single-tier Tier 1 baseline at 28.29\% (95\% CI 26.20--30.48). While single-tier Tier 1 reaches 69.01\% self-resolution accuracy, it leaves 59.00\% of events unaddressed; in contrast, the two-tier system leaves no event unresolved, at the cost of autonomous precision: 57.72\% against 69.01\%, with 55.24\% autonomous actions and 44.76\% explicit deferrals. \section{Infrastructure Scalability Analysis}
\label{sec:scalability_analysis}

Although the empirical measurements reported throughout this thesis were gathered on a single-GPU workstation, SENTINEL's two-tier architecture provides a concrete architectural foundation to scale horizontally to enterprise production environments processing billions of events per day. To address how this prototype scales to production workloads, this section delineates between the empirical baselines established in this thesis and the literature-grounded architectural projections required for enterprise throughput.

Quantitatively, an enterprise telemetry workload of 1 billion events/day corresponds to a continuous mean throughput of $\approx 11,574$ events/second, with peak surges reaching $30,000$ to $50,000$ events/second during volumetric attack campaigns (such as distributed denial-of-service or distributed scanning). System scalability is substantiated across three architectural layers:

First, the stateless execution and linear compute complexity of Tier 1: application-layer static filtering, the $\mathcal{O}(1)$ Welford statistical model~\cite{welford1962}, and the LightGBM Machine Learning Gate~\cite{ke2017lightgbm} operate entirely on CPU cores with zero GPU VRAM consumption. With empirical throughput measured at 3,860 events/second on a single CPU core (Table~\ref{tab:throughput_baselines}), absorbing an enterprise baseline of 11,574 events/second requires only 3 to 4 distributed worker cores managed via standard message streaming partitions (e.g., Kafka Consumer Groups or Kubernetes Horizontal Pod Autoscaling). This projection is linear because Tier 1 maintains zero shared mutable state between events, eliminating lock contention. Furthermore, reimplementing Tier 1 in low-level systems programming languages (such as Rust or Go utilizing DPDK kernel-bypass frameworks to eliminate Linux socket buffer copying) provides an engineering projection exceeding $100,000$ events/second on a single physical host, as documented in high-performance packet processing literature.

Second, the compounding offload efficiency of Filtration Economics and Drain3 clustering~\cite{he2017drain}: because Tier 1 empirically eliminates 97.5\% of benign and commodity events (Table~\ref{tab:tier1_performance}), escalated telemetry reaching Tier 2 is throttled to 2.5\%, or approximately 25 million events/day ($\approx 289$ events/second). At Tier 2, the Drain3 log template miner~\cite{he2017drain} clusters recurring event formats (typical of repetitive scanning or automated probing) with compression ratios between $10\times$ and $50\times$, as repetitive payload patterns collapse into fixed parse trees. Consequently, the volume of unique events requiring deep cognitive inference by the language model is compressed down to a manageable 6 to 25 events/second.

Third, the architectural migration path for Tier 2 from workstation prototypes to enterprise clusters: the single-GPU sequential inference setup using \texttt{llama.cpp} serves as a minimal proof-of-concept environment ($0.2 - 0.5$ events/second with a median latency of 13,293\,ms). In this single-threaded prototype (batch size $b=1$), generation is strictly memory-bandwidth bound: the GPU spends the vast majority of execution time transferring the 8B model weights from VRAM to compute cores, resulting in low Tensor Core arithmetic utilization (<15\%). To satisfy an enterprise workload of 6 to 25 events/second, Tier 2 is scaled through two principled software engineering deployments:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Transition to High-Throughput Batching Inference Engines:} Migrating from sequential single-request execution to serving frameworks supporting PagedAttention and Continuous Batching, such as vLLM~\cite{kwon2023vllm}. In the seminal benchmarks published by Kwon et al.~\cite{kwon2023vllm} at ACM SOSP 2023, PagedAttention eliminates internal memory fragmentation (dropping waste from 60--80\% down to under 4\%) and dynamically packs tokens from concurrent request streams into single matrix multiplications. This shifts the inference bottleneck from memory bandwidth to compute cores, delivering an empirical throughput improvement of $15\times$ to $24\times$ over naive sequential serving for 7B--13B class models under realistic arrival traffic. Distributing this workload across a commodity multi-GPU node (e.g., 2 to 4 NVIDIA RTX 4090 or A10G GPUs with tensor parallelism) reliably provides the necessary aggregate capacity of 25 to 50 events/second. It is important to emphasize that this speedup represents an architectural projection derived from peer-reviewed literature benchmarks~\cite{kwon2023vllm}, rather than an in-situ empirical measurement on a physical enterprise cluster; physical validation under production multi-GPU load remains an ongoing engineering objective.
    \item \textbf{Tiered Priority Queue Architecture:} Implementing an asynchronous message queue where critical-severity alerts (identified by extreme statistical anomaly scores from Tier 1) bypass standard buffers via a Fast Lane for immediate cognitive investigation, while ambiguous low-severity alerts are ingested via background batch processing. This ensures that critical incident response latency remains strictly bounded under burst traffic without overwhelming GPU memory.
\end{enumerate}

In summary, the empirical evaluation reported above substantiates the practical utility of SENTINEL against the operational constraints of modern Security Operations Centers. Three architectural pillars carry that result---(1) high-speed filtration economics at Tier 1 that offloads 97.5\% of raw telemetry, reduces median latency to 0.88\,ms, and achieves a throughput of 3,860 events/second; (2) deterministic adversarial guardrails that resisted all 678 hard prompt-injection samples through Delimited Data Encapsulation, constrained feature-space evasion, and sealed the forensic record with HMAC-SHA256 chaining~\cite{hmac1997}; and (3) evidence-anchored agentic cognitive reasoning at Tier 2 that isolates threats with 95.0\% recall while reducing analyst workload by 84.24\%---SENTINEL provides a reproducible basis for deploying agentic AI within enterprise security monitoring, subject to the limits reported above.
