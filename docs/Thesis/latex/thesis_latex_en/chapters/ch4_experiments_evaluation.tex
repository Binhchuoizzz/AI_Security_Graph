\chapter{Experiments and Evaluation}
\label{ch:experiments_evaluation}

This chapter presents the quantitative experimental results for the SENTINEL system, organized to follow the three research questions posed in Chapter 1. Each section states the measured metric, the denominator of the measurement, and the limits of the conclusion it supports.

\section{Experimental Setup and Measurement Conventions}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

The system is deployed on a local server representative of small-to-medium enterprise SOC infrastructure, with all microservices containerized via Docker Compose in an air-gapped environment. The governing methodological principle is that \textbf{the denominator must match the question}: distribution-dependent metrics (offload rate) are measured on the live stream, while classification quality is measured on a class-balanced set. Conflating the two is a serious source of error---measuring offload on a forced 50/50 set yields an artificially low figure, because that set does not reflect the distribution the system actually encounters. Table~\ref{tab:experimental_setup} states each dataset's role.

Two classes of check are reported outside the metric tables because they are \textbf{functional tests, not measurements}: a tier-capability audit confirming that each tier is reachable and behaves as specified (15 of 15 checks pass), and an offload-mechanism audit confirming that each documented offload path actually fires (14 of 14). They answer ``is the plumbing connected'' rather than ``how well does it work'', and reporting a pass rate alongside genuine performance figures would inflate the latter by association.

Two further conventions apply to every run. First, dynamic rules are \textbf{frozen} during measurement, preventing a run from generating rules and then benefiting from them later. Second, the 50 author-composed samples embedded in \texttt{ground\_truth.json} are excluded from all ratios---if retained, they constitute 16.7\% of the attribution scoring set and all share a single answer, skewing the label distribution.

\section{RQ1 --- Offloading and Filtration Economics}
\label{sec:tier1_performance}

This section reports \textbf{Objective 1 / RQ1} of Section~\ref{sec:rq}, under the measurement principles of Section~\ref{sec:methodology}. RQ1 has three components: offloading at wire-speed, reducing latency, and reducing resource consumption. As stated in Section~\ref{sec:architecture_overview}, an event counts as \textbf{offloaded} when it generates no language model invocation---every action other than \texttt{ESCALATE} terminates at Tier 1. This definition matters because a counting scheme crediting only \texttt{DROP} omits \texttt{BLOCK\_IP}, \texttt{ALERT}, and \texttt{AWAIT\_HITL}, none of which consume a single token.

Table~\ref{tab:tier1_performance} reveals a property not stated explicitly in the related literature: \textbf{the offload rate is a function of the stream's attack base rate, not a constant of the system}. Within a single stream the aggregate decomposes linearly as $\text{offload}(p) = (1-p)\cdot\text{offload}_{\text{benign}} + p\cdot\text{offload}_{\text{attack}}$, where $p$ is the attack rate. On the benchmark stream the two per-label rates are \textbf{92.35\%} on benign cases and \textbf{86.72\%} on attack cases, which reproduce the measured 90.6\% exactly; on the SOC-like stream they are \textbf{97.88\%} and \textbf{93.77\%}, reproducing 97.5\%. The coefficients themselves differ between the two streams because the streams differ in composition, so the relation licenses projection to a different base rate \textbf{within a stream}---not transfer of one stream's coefficients onto another. The 90.6\% figure is therefore \textbf{not an architectural ceiling} but a consequence of a test stream deliberately loaded with more than three times the realistic attack proportion.

A second observation: \textbf{the tier carrying the load changes with the stream}. On the benchmark stream, Tier 1 rules resolve 80.6\%; on the benign-dominated stream, the ML Gate becomes the primary carrier at 59.2\%. The two tiers compensate for one another rather than overlapping---empirical justification for retaining both. Figure~\ref{fig:offload_funnel} makes the inversion visible.

\textbf{Per-component savings.} The ML Gate processes each feature vector in \textbf{0.287\,ms}, achieving \textbf{3,452 events/second}. The Tier 1.75 Semantic Cache achieves an \textbf{81.3\%} hit rate (1,220 of 1,500 queries) with an \textbf{8.9$\times$} speed-up and zero evictions; its key is a hash of the normalised query string---exact match, not embedding similarity---so hits arise because many distinct events reduce to the same service/port/technique description. One quality figure belongs in this section rather than beside it, because it is the guard condition on the offload claim itself: a 90.6\% offload rate would read equally well as "passes almost everything" if the offloaded portion were not adjudicated correctly. On \texttt{datatest.json} the Gate attains \textbf{MCC 0.667} and Balanced Accuracy 0.833---MCC deliberately in place of Accuracy, since a detector answering "benign" everywhere already scores \textbf{90.2\%} Accuracy on the SOC-like stream while discriminating nothing. The denominator must be stated with the figure: of 4,236 events entering, the Gate adjudicates only \textbf{2,534}, a \textbf{59.8\%} bypass rate---1,586 events lack the payload features the model requires and 116 abstain---so these quality figures describe that adjudicated subset, not the whole file. Its \textbf{autonomous blocking} subset of 962 actions achieves \textbf{100\% Precision with zero false positives}. That last condition is mandatory: offloading that blocks genuine customers is not efficiency but loss.

\textbf{Tier 2 $\rightarrow$ Tier 1 feedback loop.} A fourth offload mechanism is verified by a two-round experiment on a fixed seed. After harvesting 598 rules from the ML Gate's blocking adjudications, the escalation rate falls from \textbf{20.28\% to 17.98\%} (a relative reduction of \textbf{11.35\%}, or 594 additional events absorbed by Tier 1), while detection quality \textbf{improves} rather than degrades: MCC $+0.0378$, Recall $+0.0452$. This is noteworthy because learn-from-adjudication mechanisms typically trade accuracy for speed; here both improve. Two qualifications keep the claim proportionate. The absolute MCC stays low---$0.071 \rightarrow 0.109$---so the experiment demonstrates that the mechanism \textit{operates}, not that Tier 1 is a strong classifier. And the simulation is optimistic in two respects: the attacker does not rotate source addresses between rounds, and the analyst approves every proposed rule, with no rejection step.

\textbf{End-to-end latency.} Latency is measured on 500 events sampled strided from \texttt{build\_stream()}, preserving the real benign/attack ratio rather than forcing a balanced set. Mean end-to-end latency falls from \textbf{17,187.9\,ms} (single-tier LLM) to \textbf{5,286.7\,ms}, a reduction of \textbf{69.2\%}, with the difference statistically significant under a Mann-Whitney U test ($p \approx 9 \times 10^{-57}$). The median is far more striking: \textbf{0.88\,ms} against \textbf{17,174.7\,ms}, a factor of roughly \textbf{19,500}---because the typical event never reaches the LLM at all. Per-stage means confirm the cost asymmetry motivating the architecture: \textbf{0.182\,ms} at Tier 1, \textbf{0.924\,ms} at the ML Gate, and \textbf{22,785\,ms} when the LLM is invoked.

One result must be reported against interest: the \textbf{95th percentile is worse} for the two-tier system---\textbf{25,829\,ms} versus \textbf{21,434\,ms} for the LLM-only baseline. The cause is structural: an event that does escalate has already paid the Tier 1 and ML Gate cost before paying the LLM cost. The tail is therefore genuinely slower, and the architecture's benefit is concentrated in the median rather than distributed uniformly. Reporting mean and median without the tail would misrepresent the trade-off. Two properties of this particular run must travel with its numbers. Its own offload rate was only \textbf{76.8\%}---116 of 500 events reached the LLM, not the 90.6\% of Table~\ref{tab:tier1_performance}---because 500 strided samples never accumulate enough per-address history for the blocklist to engage. And the RAG cache registered \textbf{zero hits across 116 lookups}, so the 8.9$\times$ cache speed-up reported above is \textbf{not} contained within the 5,286.7\,ms. For resources, the thesis reports \textbf{LLM invocations avoided} (97,199 of 99,717 events) rather than a VRAM reduction percentage, because no VRAM measurement was performed. Figure~\ref{fig:latency_ecdf} shows the whole distribution rather than the summary statistics, and the shape carries the argument: the two-tier curve resolves roughly three quarters of events below two milliseconds, then flattens until it meets the baseline in the tail.

SENTINEL's deterministic path runs \textbf{more than 20,000$\times$} faster than routing every event to the language model (measured 20,531$\times$). Static signatures are faster still, at roughly three times SENTINEL's rate, which is expected---they perform no statistical or model inference. The ordering \textit{is} the architectural argument in one line: each tier costs what it costs, and the design question is how much traffic can be settled before the expensive one is reached. Two of these figures must not be combined: the 0.19\,ev/s routes each event through the \textbf{full Tier 2 agent} (797.9\,s for 150 events), whereas the 17,187.9\,ms baseline is a \textbf{bare model call} on the raw log with no token ceiling and no output schema. They describe different single-tier designs, not one measurement in two units. Finally, surge-load testing on the 99,717-event stream confirms that consumer-group lag-based backpressure keeps RAM and VRAM utilisation stable, guaranteeing continuous availability.

\textbf{Why these thresholds.} The four-band policy is not a tuned optimum but a deliberately conservative choice, and a sweep of the operating point shows what it costs. Holding the escalation and alert bands fixed, the block threshold is on a \textbf{flat region}: 0.70, 0.75, 0.80 and 0.85 produce identical aggregate quality, and only above 0.90 does recall begin to fall. Choosing 0.85 therefore buys the widest possible margin for autonomous blocking at no measured cost. Across every configuration swept, \textbf{autonomous-block precision remains 1.00}. One configuration is aggregate-better---lowering the escalation band to 0.50 raises MCC---but it does so by escalating less, which trades a headline metric for exactly the caution this design exists to preserve.

\textbf{Detecting the unseen: where the statistical tier saturates.} Synthetic anomalies were injected at magnitudes from $2\sigma$ to $100\sigma$ across seven valid flow features (210 probes per level). Every probe at every magnitude was \textbf{escalated}, so nothing passed silently. The statistical \textit{notice}, however, plateaus: the noticed rate rises from 19.52\% at $2\sigma$ to \textbf{42.86\%} at $4\sigma$ and then \textbf{stops}, unchanged through $8\sigma$, $20\sigma$ and $50\sigma$, reaching only 54.29\% at $100\sigma$. Beyond roughly four standard deviations, making an anomaly larger does not make it more visible to this tier---the limit is the feature set, not the threshold. The practical consequence is that novel behaviour reaches Tier 2 by escalation rather than by recognition, which is the correct failure mode but not a detection claim.

\section{RQ2 --- AI Security Guardrails and Forensic Integrity}
\label{sec:adversarial_security_hmac}

This section reports \textbf{Objective 2 / RQ2}. \textbf{Threat model and measurement convention.} RQ2 names log-substrate prompt injection as a \textit{typical} adversarial risk, not as the scope. SENTINEL is therefore evaluated against \textbf{two structurally different attack surfaces}. The first attacks \textbf{through language}: injection and jailbreak text embedded in telemetry fields, aimed at the Tier 2 language model; resistance means the embedded instruction fails to change the agent's action. The second attacks \textbf{through feature space}: extreme or infinite values injected into flow attributes to force the LightGBM Gate to answer "benign"; resistance means a distorted sample is still not flipped to benign. A defence covering only the first surface is a narrow defence, since the two tiers are attacked by entirely different means.

Three conventions make the ratios interpretable. First, the static verdict is the logical OR of \textbf{three separate detectors}---pattern matching, encoding neutralisation, and delimiter stripping---each recorded individually, so that a failure can be \textit{located} rather than merely counted. Second, a \textbf{negative control is mandatory}: the same detectors run over 230 benign logs, because a layer that flags everything also achieves "100\% blocking", and a block rate quoted without its false-flag rate is uninterpretable. Third, following Section~\ref{sec:experimental_setup}, published-corpus and author-composed samples are \textbf{scored separately and never merged into one headline}. Table~\ref{tab:rq2_security} states each guardrail layer with the denominator its result is measured against.

\textbf{Tier 1 against language.} On published corpora the static pre-filter blocks \textbf{18.8\%}, and that figure must be read together with the \textbf{20.0\%} false-flag rate on benign logs. Per-detector analysis localises the problem precisely: all 46 false flags originate in the encoding-detection layer, while pattern matching and delimiter stripping produce none---an actionable finding about one layer, not a verdict on the guardrail. The cost of a false flag is also bounded and must be stated, since 20\% sounds alarming without it: a static flag raises the isolation level and annotates the record, but does \textbf{not} drop the event or block the source. It is therefore a rate of unnecessary annotation, not of benign traffic denied service. The author-composed subset blocks at 72.5\%, far above the published figure, and the gap is explained rather than excused: 45 of those 80 samples are encoding-bypass cases and the encoding neutraliser was built precisely for them. Reporting the merged 23.3\% would have flattered the system by counting samples written with knowledge of the defence; \textbf{18.8\% is the figure the thesis stands behind}. Figure~\ref{fig:guardrail_rates} places all four rates on one axis, so the block rate cannot be read without its negative control.

\textbf{Tier 1 against feature space.} The ML Gate clips $Z$-scores to $\pm 8\sigma$, imputes missing values from the baseline, and abstains when the distorted-feature proportion exceeds 30\%. Under the \textbf{hard mode}, in which \textit{every} feature of a detected attack is driven to an extreme, \textbf{98.75\%} of samples resist (1,023 of 1,036; 95\% CI 97.86--99.27), with 13 flipped to benign. The two single-feature modes reach 100\%, and the thesis cites the hard mode rather than an average across all three, because averaging in two trivially saturated modes would inflate the figure past what the defence has actually demonstrated. Abstention is counted as a guardrail outcome, not a failure: 116 events were escalated instead of guessed, since a confident wrong answer causes autonomous action with no one reviewing.

\textbf{Tier 2 and the categorical difference.} The static layer is an inexpensive pre-filter; the security guarantee comes from \textbf{Delimited Data Encapsulation}, which is structural---content inside the nonce-bounded region is processed as passive text \textit{regardless of what it says}. It was evaluated on \textbf{all 678 samples of the seven categories the static layer does not absorb}, at \textbf{100\% coverage} of that set. This is a superset of the 543 samples that genuinely bypassed the static layer within those categories, so the test is stricter than required rather than favourably sampled; Tier 2 resisted \textbf{678 of 678}, of which 676 (99.7\%) produced an independent language-model call and two collapsed onto a shared cache fingerprint. That is the categorical difference: a classifier blocking $x\%$ always leaks $(100-x)\%$ and degrades against novel variants, whereas a structural guarantee does not depend on recognising malicious content at all. The 18.8\% therefore \textbf{does not contradict} the thesis---it quantifies how much work the structural layer must absorb. Two robustness controls support every LLM-dependent measurement in this chapter, and each must be reported with its limit. Under a fixed seed the agent emitted \textbf{identical actions} across five runs---but on a \textbf{single} sample. Varying only the seed across ten samples flipped \textbf{no adjudication}; at $n = 10$ that bounds the flip rate only at \textbf{0.2775} (95\% CI upper), and all ten returned \texttt{AWAIT\_HITL} at every seed, so the measurement is saturated on one action and resolves little. These controls therefore make it \textit{unlikely}---not certain---that differences between configurations are decoder noise. A forced backend failure degraded to \texttt{AWAIT\_HITL} rather than crashing or defaulting to a permissive action.

\textbf{How tampering is detected.} Verification recomputes the entire chain from the genesis record under $H_i = \text{HMAC}(D_i \parallel H_{i-1}, K)$. Because each link consumes its predecessor's hash, a single byte altered in $D_j$ invalidates \textit{every} link from $j$ onward, so detection is \textbf{positional} rather than a bare "something changed". The three core modes map onto the three ways continuity can be broken: a \textbf{content edit} makes the recomputed hash at $j$ disagree with the stored one; an \textbf{inserted forged record} has no valid predecessor to chain back to; a \textbf{deleted middle record} leaves $H_{j+1}$ referencing a hash that no longer exists. All three are detected in 30 of 30 trials, and the negative control over 450 intact records raises no false alarm. The measurement ran with a key loaded from the environment, not the in-source default---a key visible in source would let the whole chain be forged from scratch and would void every rate above. The thesis states the blind spot proactively: \textbf{tail truncation is undetectable}, an inherent property of log-chaining, since deleting trailing records leaves no subsequent link against which to verify. The remedy is periodic anchoring of hash values outside the system.

\textbf{Guardrails that do not reduce to a single ratio.} Several controls defend paths that no block-rate captures. The \texttt{RAG\_\allowbreak Sanitizer} guards the knowledge base at both ingestion---NFKC normalisation against homoglyphs, removal of control and zero-width characters, HTML/JS and Markdown stripping---and retrieval, neutralising imperative instructions embedded in retrieved documents. Its design carries a measured lesson: an earlier revision matched blocked phrases anywhere in a sentence and consequently rewrote legitimate security prose, neutralising four lateral-movement entries (\texttt{T1090}, \texttt{T1021}, \texttt{T1021.001}, \texttt{T1553.001}) that the thesis's own APT scenarios depend on. Restricting matches to \textbf{imperative position} eliminated this, and re-verification over the current 433-entry knowledge base yields \textbf{zero} false neutralisations---the same block-rate-versus-false-flag discipline applied to a layer that publishes no ratio. The \texttt{Feedback\allowbreak Validator} closes the loop opened by the Tier 2 to Tier 1 feedback mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:tier1_performance}: rules promoted from adjudications pass a field allowlist, pattern validation, and score bounds, because a mechanism that learns from verdicts is also a mechanism for poisoning Tier 1 rules. The \texttt{Decision\allowbreak Validator} enforces the output schema, salvages malformed fields by regular expression, and requires \textbf{Tier 1 concurrence} before any autonomous block. A loop detector caps node revisits at ten to stop an agent from circling and consuming GPU time, and an output sanitiser strips base64 and hexadecimal blobs before records reach the database or the dashboard, closing the re-emission path back through the analyst interface.

\textbf{Field position, tested separately.} Because nonce encapsulation wraps \textbf{per field}, field position decides whether a payload falls inside or outside the wrapped region---a property no aggregate figure can test, since the other categories all place their payload in the same field. The \texttt{field\_\allowbreak injection} set of 100 published samples distributes payloads across four positions: URI 24, User-Agent 23, message 30, payload 23. The static layer blocks only 12 of 100, as expected for plain-language injection carrying neither encoding nor delimiters. Run through Tier 2 at \textbf{100\% coverage}, all 100 resisted, with adjudications distributed as 73 \texttt{BLOCK\_IP} and 27 \texttt{ALERT} and none degraded to a permissive action.

\textbf{The per-position claim required a second measurement, and the reason is instructive.} On the first run the aggregate looked conclusive and the per-position breakdown looked uniform---but the 100 samples had produced only \textbf{79 independent language-model adjudications}. The second-layer cache key was built from \texttt{message}, \texttt{payload} and \texttt{uri} and omitted \texttt{user\_\allowbreak agent}, so the 23 User-Agent samples collapsed onto \textbf{two} fingerprints: two verdicts replayed twenty-one times, in exactly the column the experiment existed to test. A defect in the measurement apparatus and a live cache-poisoning surface were here the same defect, since an attacker in production could equally steer a field absent from the key and be served a stored verdict. Widening the key to cover every application-layer field restored 100 distinct fingerprints, and the experiment was re-run on that basis.

The re-measured result is \textbf{uniform across all four positions}---24/24, 23/23, 30/30 and 23/23---now backed by one hundred independent adjudications rather than seventy-nine. The uniformity, not the total, is the finding: had encapsulation been applied to some fields and not others, the unwrapped positions would have surfaced here as a gap. This is reported separately from the 678 because it answers a different question, and the episode is reported at all because the corrected figure is identical to the uncorrected one---which is precisely why a denominator has to be verified rather than assumed.

\section{RQ3 --- Stateful Reasoning and Attribution}
\label{sec:tier2_accuracy_rag}

This section reports \textbf{Objective 3 / RQ3}. RQ3 has four components: ATT\&CK technique attribution, knowledge retrieval, transparent reporting, and analyst workload reduction. Each is measured separately below, because they fail for different reasons and one aggregate score would hide that.

\textbf{Attribution: adding the reasoning tier makes it worse.} Attribution is measured twice on an \textbf{identical population}---500 payload-layer samples drawn from \texttt{ground\_truth.json} after the author-composed exclusion, comprising 250 samples carrying a technique label and 250 benign, sourced from CSIC 2010. The first pass runs \textbf{retrieval only}: RRF fusion decides the technique with no language model in the loop. The second runs the \textbf{full agent}. Exact-match attribution falls from \textbf{80.0\%} to \textbf{68.0\%}, and tactic-level from 80.0\% to 70.4\%, while median latency rises from \textbf{0.54\,ms} to \textbf{13,293\,ms}. The reasoning tier costs four orders of magnitude in time and returns twelve points of accuracy \textit{less} than the retrieval layer it was added to improve.

The loss is explained rather than excused. Of the 30 samples lost, up to \textbf{22} are cases where the evidence-anchoring guardrail forced the verdict to \texttt{N/A} because the technique the model proposed was not present in that batch's retrieved documents. That is a deliberate trade, not a malfunction: the system exchanges attribution accuracy for the guarantee that it never acts on an unsupported identifier. Any figure here must also be read against a floor rather than against zero---\texttt{T1595.003} accounts for 130 of the 250 labelled samples, so a system always answering that identifier already scores \textbf{52\%}.

\textbf{Knowledge retrieval and its ceiling.} On the same payload slice---243 queries, seven excluded because Tier 1 did not escalate them---Dual-RAG attains \textbf{Recall@3 = 0.930} (95\% CI 0.891--0.956), MRR 0.803, and a median rank of 1 when the correct document is found. That is the genuine ceiling of the attribution figures above, and it sits comfortably over them, so the argument closes: retrieval is not what limits the 80.0\%. The unfavourable half must be stated with equal clarity. Measured instead over the whole \texttt{ground\_truth} set of 1,305 queries, Recall@3 collapses to \textbf{0.385}, because flow-only samples dominate that population and carry no application-layer text to match against---\texttt{T1110} scores hit@3 of \textbf{0.00} across 236 queries. The two numbers describe \textbf{different populations} and 0.385 must never be set against the 80.0\%; comparing them would breach the very principle established in Section~\ref{sec:experimental_setup}.

\textbf{The anchoring guardrail, measured from execution traces.} The evidence-anchoring guardrail (Section~\ref{sec:dual_rag}) requires every emitted technique identifier to appear in that batch's retrieved documents; violations are forced to \texttt{N/A} and routed to \texttt{AWAIT\_HITL}. Verifying it demands care, because reading only final verdicts would score \textit{any} shield at zero hallucinations---including one that does nothing. The measurement therefore runs on execution traces that record the action \textit{before} and \textit{after} the shield: \textbf{1,566 batches}, of which 1,421 asserted a technique. The result is \textbf{zero} unanchored assertions (0 of 1,421), and zero even when relaxed to the parent technique. That the shield is doing real work is shown by its activation count: it fired \textbf{145 times (9.26\%)}, and in \textbf{76} of those it downgraded \texttt{BLOCK\_IP} to \texttt{AWAIT\_HITL}---meaning \textbf{76 automatic, permanent IP blocks were prevented} because the justification could not be anchored. All 76 originated in a technique the model itself proposed; a further 69 activations caught identifiers the mapper had inferred. The most frequently rejected identifiers were \texttt{T1218} (27), \texttt{T1083} (24), \texttt{T1071.001} (16) and \texttt{T1190} (14). One limit is stated proactively: this score uses the \textbf{strict} criterion---the identifier must be among the retrieved document IDs---whereas the deployed shield matches by regular expression across the full context text, so zero is an \textbf{upper bound} on shield leakage rather than an exact count.

\textbf{Triaging the work that reaches a human.} This is the component that answers RQ3 most directly, and it produces the chapter's most negative result. Tier 1 escalated \textbf{1,066 alerts}, of which only \textbf{80 (7.5\%) were genuine threats}; the population is dominated by 910 benign CSIC requests, alongside 75 \texttt{cicids}, 76 \texttt{cicids\_max}, 4 zero-day and 1 adversarial sample. As a binary classifier over that queue the language model is \textbf{worthless}: it flagged all 1,066, dismissed \textbf{none} of the 986 false alarms, and therefore scores \textbf{MCC 0.0}, specificity \textbf{0.0} and Balanced Accuracy \textbf{0.5}. Within the subset it actively confirmed, the false-positive rate is \textbf{99.55\%} (894 of 898; 95\% CI 98.86--99.83). Its confidence is not merely uninformative but actively misleading---mean confidence when flagging was \textbf{0.846} against a true rate of 7.5\%, an expected calibration error of \textbf{0.803}, and the 900 highest-confidence adjudications were correct \textbf{zero} times.

The value sits elsewhere, in the channel the binary framing ignores. The agent deferred \textbf{168 alerts} to \texttt{AWAIT\_HITL}---\textbf{15.76\%} of the input---and that queue contains \textbf{76 of the 80 genuine threats (95.0\%)}, at a true-alert density of \textbf{45.24\%} against 7.5\% at the input. That is a \textbf{6.03$\times$ enrichment}: an analyst who reviews only the deferred queue examines \textbf{84.24\% less} material while still seeing 95.0\% of the real threats. The operational conclusion follows directly and is stated as a finding rather than a defence: this model is usable as a \textbf{triage router} and is not usable as an autonomous confirmer. Nor does MCC 0.0 contradict RQ3 as posed, which asks for reduction and \textit{accurate triage} of the work requiring a human, not for correct verdicts on every alert---and since 910 of the 1,066 escalations were benign CSIC traffic, the figure also reflects the quality of Tier 1's escalation decision, not the agent's alone. For completeness of denominator: 68 of the 1,066 batches were served from cache, leaving \textbf{998} independent language-model calls. \textbf{Reasoning quality.} Agent justifications are graded by a \textbf{cross-family judge} (Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct scoring Foundation-Sec-8B-Instruct) on a 1--5 scale along the four axes of the RAGAS retrieval-augmented evaluation framework~\cite{ragas2023}, following the recommended protocol for eliminating Self-Enhancement Bias~\cite{zheng2023judge}. The run covers all \textbf{1,052} escalated events and is clean in the respect that matters for interpretation: \textbf{no adjudication was missing a required field}, and the 71 cases returning no technique identifier were \textbf{deliberate abstentions} enforced by the anchoring guardrail rather than parse failures. The four axes score: context precision \textbf{2.54}; answer relevancy \textbf{4.52}; faithfulness \textbf{3.96}; context recall \textbf{4.11}; mean \textbf{3.78/5.0}.

Two results run against the system and are reported as such. \textbf{Context precision at 2.54 is the weakest axis}, corroborating the retrieval diagnosis above: the retrieved set frequently carries entries irrelevant to the event at hand, and the bottleneck is therefore in retrieval rather than in generation. More seriously, \textbf{evidence grounding reaches only 11.2\%} (95\% CI 9.5--13.3), averaging \textbf{0.2} verifiable citations per justification---roughly nine justifications in ten quote no log value that can be checked back against the record. This is the sharpest gap in the architecture between what is \textit{guaranteed} and what is \textit{volunteered}: the anchoring guardrail can prove that an asserted ATT\&CK identifier is supported by retrieved documents, but nothing compels the surrounding prose to cite the specific field values that led there. Transparent forensic reporting, the third component of RQ3, is therefore only partially achieved, and closing that gap is a matter of constraining the justification format rather than of improving the model.

\textbf{Component ablation: what each tier actually contributes.} A configuration comparison closes the loop between the design of Chapter~\ref{ch:system_design_implementation} and the results reported here. Six configurations are scored on \texttt{ground\_truth.json}, and the choice of metric has to be justified before the numbers can be read. The binary framing---did the system flag the event or not---is \textbf{unusable} on this set: the attack base rate is 86.9\%, so every configuration scores close to that figure and the comparison collapses. All configurations previously appeared identical for exactly this reason. Scoring instead on the \textbf{final action}---whether the emitted verdict matches the expected one across \texttt{BLOCK\_IP}, \texttt{ALERT}, \texttt{LOG} and \texttt{AWAIT\_HITL}---restores the discrimination the binary metric destroyed. The change of metric is reported here rather than passed over, because a metric selected after seeing results is a metric worth distrusting; the justification is that the binary version is degenerate on this distribution, not that it was unflattering.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation_actions} reports the six configurations. Three findings follow, and the second is the one that matters. First, the reasoning tier earns its cost: the full system reaches \textbf{35.35\%} action accuracy against \textbf{28.29\%} for Tier 1 alone, and the confidence intervals do not overlap, so the gap is not sampling noise. Second, and more informative than the headline, \textbf{the trade-off inverts}. Tier 1 alone is the \textit{more} reliable adjudicator whenever it commits---\textbf{69.01\%} autonomous precision against \textbf{57.72\%}---but it commits on only 41.00\% of events and leaves \textbf{59.00\% unresolved}, escalating them into a tier that, in configuration A, does not exist. The full system leaves nothing unresolved; it converts that backlog into \textbf{44.76\%} explicit deferrals to an analyst. The two tiers therefore do not differ in how well they think so much as in \textbf{what they do when they do not know}, which is precisely the property Section~\ref{sec:tier2_accuracy_rag} measured from the other direction in the deferral channel.

Third, a null result reported as such: \textbf{C, D and E are identical to the digit}. Adding sparse retrieval to dense, and then fusing the two rankings, changes no adjudication on this slice. This is a limitation of the ablation design rather than evidence that the retrieval ladder is inert---all three configurations invoke the language model only on events Tier 1 escalates, so the large majority of the slice falls through to the same \texttt{tier1\_verdict} regardless of what the retriever would have returned. Isolating the contribution of rank fusion requires an evaluation restricted to escalated events, which is what the retrieval measurement above provides and this ablation does not.

\textbf{Multi-stage correlation, at the scale the evidence supports.} Stateful correlation across a campaign was exercised on nine DAPT2020 chains: all \textbf{3 of 3} genuine multi-day chains were reconstructed, and none of the \textbf{4} benign multi-day sequences triggered a false campaign alert. The result is reported for completeness and claims nothing: with three positives the 95\% confidence interval on recall runs from \textbf{0.44 to 1.00}, which is compatible with a system that works and with one that was lucky. Consistent with Section~\ref{sec:rq}, multi-stage correlation remains a proof-of-concept capability and is carried into future work rather than counted as a contribution.

\textbf{Division of labour between tiers.} A seemingly negative result confirms the design: on the non-NetFlow sample group (CSIC web attacks, zero-day, DAPT), the ML Gate attains only \textbf{11.1\% Recall}, and 305 of the full set's 336 misses fall entirely within this group. The cause is architectural rather than defective---the ML Gate is trained on \textbf{flow features}, whereas web attacks reside in the \textbf{payload}, which is exactly the work assigned to Tier 2 and the signature set. The methodological consequence is that the aggregate Recall of 0.755 mixes two non-additive populations and must be reported separately. Relatedly, and as stated in Section~\ref{sec:tier2_agent_fsm}, the escalation-to-analyst rate is \textbf{not} a quantity to be minimised: an architecture claiming a zero human-review rate under all conditions has discarded its final guardrail.
