\chapter{Experiments and Evaluation}
\label{ch:experiments_evaluation}

This chapter presents the comprehensive empirical evaluation of the SENTINEL system across three core dimensions: wire-speed stream offloading performance, adversarial AI security guardrails with cryptographic audit integrity, and stateful agentic reasoning quality. Experiments were executed on an air-gapped server environment containerized via Docker Compose (Intel Core i7, 32\,GB RAM, local NVIDIA GPU running CUDA \texttt{llama.cpp} with Foundation-Sec-8B-Instruct~\cite{foundation_sec_instruct}). Following Section~\ref{sec:methodology}, offloading performance is evaluated on an operational-scale telemetry stream of 99,717 events with a 9.8\% attack base rate reflecting near-realistic SOC conditions~\cite{alahmadi2022}. Classification quality and security guardrails are assessed across benchmark datasets CSE-CIC-IDS2018~\cite{ids2018}, CSIC 2010~\cite{csic2010}, DAPT 2020~\cite{dapt2020} and 823 adversarial samples~\cite{advbench2023, deepset2023, jackhhao2023, shen2024dan}. Dynamic rules were frozen during all evaluations, and every ratio is reported with the denominator on which it was measured. Only measurements that substantiate one of the three research questions are reported here; design-parameter verifications and proof-of-concept probes are omitted as they support no contribution claim.

\section{Stream Offloading Performance and Filtration Economics}
\label{sec:tier1_performance}

Following the definition in Section~\ref{sec:architecture_overview}, an event is offloaded when it is resolved without invoking the language model, a count that includes \texttt{BLOCK\_IP}, \texttt{ALERT} and \texttt{AWAIT\_HITL} as well as \texttt{DROP}, none of which consume inference tokens. The offload rate behaves as a function of the attack base rate of the stream rather than as a constant of the system. Within a stream it decomposes linearly as $\text{offload}(p) = (1-p)\cdot\text{offload}_{\text{benign}} + p\cdot\text{offload}_{\text{attack}}$: benchmark per-label rates of 92.35\% and 86.72\% reproduce the measured 90.6\% exactly, and the corresponding 97.88\% and 93.77\% reproduce 97.5\% on the SOC-like stream. Because the coefficients differ between streams, the relation supports projection within a stream but not transfer between streams, and the 90.6\% is therefore not a performance ceiling but the outcome of a benchmark carrying more than three times the realistic attack proportion. The tier bearing the load also inverts, from Tier 1 at 80.6\% on the benchmark stream to the Machine Learning Gate at 59.2\% on the benign-dominated one, indicating that the two tiers compensate for one another rather than overlap.

A high offload rate is meaningful only if the offloaded portion is adjudicated correctly, since indiscriminate passing would yield an identical headline figure. On \texttt{datatest.json} the Gate attains a Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) of 0.667 and a Balanced Accuracy of 0.833; MCC is preferred to Accuracy because a benign-everywhere classifier already scores 90.2\% Accuracy on the SOC-like stream. The denominator travels with the metric: of 4,236 events the Gate adjudicates 2,534, as 1,586 lack payload features and 116 are abstained to Tier 2. Its 962 autonomous blocking decisions achieve 100\% Precision with zero false positives, an operational prerequisite rather than an incidental result, since blocking legitimate traffic converts efficiency into service disruption. Feature-vector inference completes in 0.287\,ms (3,452 events per second), and the Tier 1.75 exact-match cache resolves 81.3\% of 1,500 repeated queries at an 8.9$\times$ speed-up.

Latency was measured on 500 events sampled at fixed stride from the benchmark stream. Mean end-to-end latency falls from 17,187.9\,ms to 5,286.7\,ms ($-69.2\%$; Mann--Whitney $U$, $p \approx 9 \times 10^{-57}$) and median latency from 17,174.7\,ms to 0.88\,ms, a factor of approximately 19,500, because the typical event never reaches the model. The 95th percentile is nonetheless worse under the two-tier design, at 25,829\,ms against 21,434\,ms, since an escalating event incurs the Tier 1 and Gate cost before the model cost: the advantage lies in the body of the distribution rather than the tail, as the Empirical Cumulative Distribution Function (ECDF) in Figure~\ref{fig:latency_ecdf} shows. Resource saving is expressed as invocations avoided, 97,199 of 99,717 events, rather than as a VRAM percentage, since no VRAM measurement was performed.

\section{Adversarial AI Guardrails and Cryptographic Audit Integrity}
\label{sec:adversarial_security_hmac}

Research Question 2 (RQ2) treats log-substrate prompt injection as a representative rather than exhaustive risk vector, so two structurally distinct surfaces are evaluated: attack through language, where resistance means an embedded instruction fails to alter the action of the agent, and attack through feature space, where resistance means a distorted sample is not reclassified as benign. Three conventions govern the ratios. The static verdict is the logical disjunction of three detectors recorded individually, so a failure can be localised; a negative control over benign logs is mandatory, since a layer flagging every input also blocks every input; and published-corpus samples are never merged with author-composed ones. Table~\ref{tab:rq2_security} states each guardrail layer with its measurement denominator.

The static pre-filter blocks 18.8\% of the published corpora, which must be read together with the 20.0\% false-flag rate measured on 230 benign logs. All 46 false flags originate in the encoding-detection layer, and their operational cost is bounded: a static flag raises the isolation level and annotates the record but neither drops the event nor blocks the source, making it a rate of unnecessary annotation rather than of legitimate traffic denied service. The author-composed subset blocks at 72.5\% because 45 of those 80 samples are encoding-bypass cases the neutraliser was designed to address; the merged rate of 23.3\% would overstate efficacy by counting samples authored with prior knowledge of the static rules, so 18.8\% is presented as the authoritative metric.

Under the hard evasion mode, in which every feature of a detected attack is driven to an extreme, 98.75\% of samples resist reclassification; the two single-feature modes reach 100\%, and the hard mode is cited rather than an average because averaging across two trivially saturated modes would inflate the figure beyond what the defence demonstrates. The structural guarantee derives from Delimited Data Encapsulation, evaluated at 100\% coverage over all 678 samples of the seven categories the static layer does not absorb, a superset of the 543 that genuinely bypassed it, so the test is stricter than required rather than favourably sampled. Tier 2 resisted 678 of 678. Unlike a probabilistic classifier, which necessarily leaks whatever it fails to recognise, this guarantee does not depend on recognising malicious content at all. Because encapsulation is applied per field, field position is verified separately: across 100 published samples distributed over URI, User-Agent, message and payload, Tier 2 resisted every sample uniformly across the four positions.

Forensic integrity was verified by recomputing the chain from the genesis record: because each link consumes the hash of its predecessor, a single altered byte in $D_j$ invalidates every link from $j$ onward, rendering detection positional rather than a generic notification that something changed. The three core tampering modes, namely content edit, forged insertion, and deletion of an intermediate record, are each detected in 30 of 30 trials, with no false alarm across 450 intact records. The secret key was loaded from the environment rather than the in-source default, as a key visible in source would permit forgery of the entire chain and invalidate every rate above. Tail truncation remains undetectable, an inherent property of sequential log chaining; the remedy is periodic anchoring of hash values outside the system.

\section{Agentic Cognitive Reasoning and Attack Technique Attribution}
\label{sec:tier2_accuracy_rag}

The dimensions evaluated under Research Question 3 (RQ3), namely technique attribution, retrieval efficacy, transparent reporting, and analyst workload reduction, are measured independently because they fail for distinct reasons. Attribution was evaluated on 500 payload-layer samples drawn from CSIC 2010, comprising 250 labelled and 250 benign records. Retrieval alone attributes 80.0\% of techniques exactly, whereas the complete agentic pipeline attains 68.0\%, with tactic-level accuracy falling from 80.0\% to 70.4\% and median latency rising from 0.54\,ms to 13,293\,ms. The reasoning tier therefore costs four orders of magnitude in latency and returns lower attribution accuracy than the retrieval layer it was introduced to improve. Of the 30 samples lost, as many as 22 are cases in which the anchoring guardrail forced \texttt{N/A} because the proposed technique was absent from the retrieved documents of that batch, a deliberate exchange of accuracy for the guarantee that no unsupported identifier is acted upon. Every figure here reads against a majority-class floor rather than zero: \texttt{T1595.003} accounts for 130 of the 250 labelled samples, so constant prediction already scores 52\%.

On the same slice of 243 escalated queries, Dual-RAG attains a Recall@3 of 0.930 (95\% CI 0.891--0.956) and a Mean Reciprocal Rank of 0.803, above the attribution figures, confirming that retrieval is not what limits the 80.0\%. The unfavourable half is equally explicit: across the full \texttt{ground\_truth} set of 1,305 queries Recall@3 falls to 0.385, because flow-only samples dominate that population and carry no application-layer text to match, with \texttt{T1110} recording a hit@3 of 0.00 across 236 queries. The two values describe different populations and are not interchangeable.

Evaluating only final verdicts would assign zero hallucinations to any shield, including an entirely inert one, so the measurement operates on execution traces recording the action before and after the shield. Of 1,566 batches, 1,421 asserted a technique, and none of those 1,421 was unanchored, a result that holds even when relaxed to the parent technique. The shield is demonstrably active in achieving this: it intervened on 9.26\% of batches and, in 76 of them, downgraded \texttt{BLOCK\_IP} to \texttt{AWAIT\_HITL}, preventing 76 automatic permanent address blocks whose justification could not be anchored. One boundary is stated proactively: the score applies the strict criterion that the identifier appear among retrieved document identifiers, whereas the deployed shield matches by regular expression across the full context, making zero an upper bound on leakage rather than an exact count.

Tier 1 escalated 1,066 alerts, of which only 80, or 7.5\%, were genuine threats. Evaluated strictly as a binary classifier over this queue, the model exhibits minimal discriminative utility: it confirmed all 1,066 events without dismissing any of the 986 benign alerts, yielding MCC 0.0, Balanced Accuracy 0.5, and a 99.55\% False Positive Rate within the subset it actively confirmed. The operational value resides in the channel the binary framing disregards. The agent deferred 168 alerts, 15.76\% of the input, and that queue contains 76 of the 80 genuine threats, or 95.0\%, at a true-alert density of 45.24\% against 7.5\% at the input: a 6.03$\times$ enrichment, such that an analyst reviewing only the deferred queue examines 84.24\% less material while still encountering 95.0\% of the real threats. The model therefore operates as a triage router rather than an autonomous decision authority; and since 910 of the 1,066 escalations were benign CSIC traffic, the figure also reflects the Tier 1 escalation decision rather than the behaviour of the agent alone.

A cross-family judge, Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct scoring Foundation-Sec-8B-Instruct, graded all 1,052 escalated events on the four RAGAS axes~\cite{ragas2023}, following the recommended protocol against self-enhancement bias~\cite{zheng2023judge}: context precision 2.54, answer relevancy 4.52, faithfulness 3.96, and context recall 4.11, for a mean of 3.78 out of 5.0. Context precision is the weakest axis and corroborates the retrieval diagnosis, since the retrieved set frequently contains entries irrelevant to the event. More consequentially, evidence grounding reaches only 11.2\% (95\% CI 9.5--13.3), so approximately nine justifications in ten quote no log value verifiable against the record. This is the widest gap between what the architecture guarantees and what it volunteers: anchoring proves that an asserted identifier is supported, but nothing compels the surrounding prose to cite the field values behind it, so transparent forensic reporting is only partially achieved.

The component ablation was scored on the final action rather than on a binary verdict, because at an 86.9\% attack base rate every configuration scores close to that figure and the comparison collapses; the emitted action was instead compared against the expected one across \texttt{BLOCK\_IP}, \texttt{ALERT}, \texttt{LOG} and \texttt{AWAIT\_HITL}. The change of measure is disclosed because post-hoc metric selection warrants methodological scrutiny, its justification being the statistical degeneracy of the binary measure on this distribution rather than its unfavourable outcome. On 1,700 samples the complete two-tier system attains 35.35\% action accuracy (95\% CI 33.12--37.66) against 28.29\% (95\% CI 26.20--30.48) for Tier 1 alone, with non-overlapping intervals. The trade-off nevertheless inverts: Tier 1 alone is the more reliable adjudicator whenever it commits, at 69.01\% autonomous precision against 57.72\%, yet it commits on only 41.00\% of events and leaves 59.00\% unresolved, escalating them to a tier that configuration A does not possess. The complete system leaves nothing unresolved, converting that backlog into 44.76\% explicit deferrals alongside 55.24\% autonomous decisions. The two configurations therefore differ less in the quality of their reasoning than in the action they execute under uncertainty, the same property the deferral channel measures from the opposite direction.
